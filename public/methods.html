WODAH · METHODS v1 · 8 NOVEMBER 2026

A terrain screen for
floodplain connection.

Wodah compares places along a user-drawn river stretch. It asks where a hypothetical flat water surface could connect to more nearby low ground. It does not calculate the water level produced by a woody barrier.

What is observed, assumed and unknown?

Observed from the supplied fileOne 5,000 × 5,000 float32 band; EPSG:27700; 1 m pixels; E275000–280000, N50000–55000; 45.836–219.071 elevation-like values; no nodata cells in the supplied raster.
Assumed for screeningValues are metres; terrain represents a surface usable for provisional connectivity; flat water surface; four-neighbour connectivity; user-selected corridor, study extent, level increments, error stress test and channel exclusion buffer.
UnknownDTM or DSM, acquisition date, vertical datum, actual vertical accuracy, source licence, submerged bed, bankfull level, flow, barrier hydraulics, asset completeness, soils and groundwater. File timestamps are not survey dates.

Reproducible calculation

  1. Draw your exact river centreline and surrounding study polygon. No restoration endpoints are prepopulated. Geometry is stored in British National Grid metres. The TIFF supplies elevation values; no river centreline is extracted from it. The drawn line places candidates, orients cross-sections and defines the channel exclusion buffer; the polygon sets the analysis extent. Candidate positions are interpolated at the chosen chainage interval (10 m minimum, at most 40 candidates). If the requested interval would exceed 40 candidates, it is increased automatically to the next 5 m step at or above stretch length / 39. The interface previews the count and effective interval; calculation updates the interval in the settings, saved setup and exports. The drawn geometry is preserved. Interval is not recommended barrier spacing. Chainage follows drawing order, not inferred flow direction.
  2. For each candidate load a square of native 1 m cells, extending ±100, 200 or 300 m. Tiles are stitched before analysis; tile seams are not boundaries. Clip eligibility to the drawn study polygon. No terrain smoothing, sink filling, channel carving, bridge removal or assumed culverts are applied. Off-channel area excludes cells within the selected distance either side of the entire drawn centreline. This is an assumed corridor mask, not measured banks or a guarantee that all submerged terrain is excluded.
  3. Reference elevation is the median of a 3 × 3 cell neighbourhood at the drawn point. All nine cells and the seed must contain valid terrain. The reference is not a measured bed, present water surface, channel depth or bankfull level. Unknown surface type may materially distort this reference.
  4. Explore reference +0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.50 and 2.00 m. These increments are exploratory and are not dam heights. Hold the reference fixed and vary water relative to terrain by −e, 0 and +e, where e is a user-chosen sensitivity (0.10–1.00 m). These are stress tests, not calibrated error estimates or statistical confidence intervals. A common datum shift of both terrain and reference cancels; the chosen relative-level perturbation tests uncertain local reference/channel-to-floodplain elevation differences.
  5. From the candidate cell, flood-fill cells at or below the assumed surface using four orthogonal neighbours. A cell requires a complete low-ground path to the seed. Diagonal corner contact alone does not permit connection. If the seed is above the surface the connected set is empty. Higher ground, disconnected low ground and nodata remain excluded. No upstream-only or flat-pond containment is inferred.
  6. Connected area = number of connected cells × 1 m². Off-channel area applies the corridor exclusion mask. Results are potential terrain connection under the assumed surface, not predicted inundation. Contact with the local window marks an open analysis edge. Contact with a study edge or nodata marks an incomplete result. Orange indicators show sampled contact locations; a dotted orange line follows a breadth-first low-terrain path to the first detected analysis-limit contact. This path proves cell connectivity only; these are not mapped hydraulic flow routes, lowest spill saddles or proof of hydraulic escape. The window outline shows the bounded search area.
  7. Rank at the selected increment by minimum off-channel area across the three sensitivity cases. Display nominal ranking and the rank range across these cases in CSV/GeoJSON and the comparison table. Ties share rank. This is a transparent area-only opportunity score: gradient, valley relief and edge flags are reported separately, not hidden in a composite weight. Prefer stable comparative performance, but examine truncation and constraints before interpreting a high rank.

Terrain shape and spill interpretation

Local corridor gradient is the absolute difference of source elevations 20 m either side along the candidate's local drawn segment tangent, divided by 40 m, expressed as percent. It is a local terrain diagnostic, not a channel hydraulic gradient. Cross-section samples are 1 m apart, perpendicular to that tangent, for ±100 m. Valley relief is the higher of the source elevations at ±50 m minus the candidate reference. It may be negative and does not measure a closed basin. Profiles can reveal potential valley confinement or high-ground thresholds. Structures, vegetation and bridges may create false constraints if this raster is a DSM.

A connection to the analysis edge is a demonstrable low-terrain route out of the bounded calculation, not proof of a real water discharge route. Study-edge and nodata contacts are unknown beyond that limit. Increase the window or expand the boundary to investigate truncation. The method does not find the minimum spill elevation, model a barrier, or establish containment even when no edge contact occurs.

Why volume is unavailable

The generic numerical core can integrate geometric void Σ max(surface − terrain, 0) × cell area for a closed, completely known synthetic basin. Wodah suppresses this quantity for the supplied site in the worker and all exports. The file's ground/surface classification and bathymetry are unknown; the river corridor buffer is only an assumption. Presenting this void as retained storage would be unsupported. Retained volume is always null/unavailable. Unknown submerged-channel capacity is never counted as measured storage.

There is no virtual barrier. Its position, permeability and extent are therefore not implicitly modelled. A connected footprint includes whatever the flat surface can reach on both sides of a point. It does not show what a woody structure could hold. Wodah does not predict actual rise, retention time, infiltration or groundwater recharge, sediment accumulation, flood-peak reduction, or optimal/approved construction positions. Footprints from different candidates can overlap and must not be summed.

Preparation, resolution and coordinates

The original TIFF is read only and is neither committed nor served. Analysis tiles retain every native horizontal cell. Elevations are rounded to centimetres (maximum added error 0.005 m), encoded as PNG R × 256 + G; code 65535 is nodata. This quantisation is much smaller than the chosen sensitivity range, but can change connectivity at exact thresholds. A 5 × 5 valid-cell mean overview is used only for map display and print context; display hillshade is illustrative. It is not used in analysis. Browser map tiles render that overview at 128 samples per 256-pixel tile; footprints display by nearest cell sampling. Exports retain exact calculated cell areas and the selected footprint as row-run cell polygons.

Map conversion uses Airy 1830 transverse Mercator and OSGB36/WGS84 seven-parameter Helmert. Expect roughly 3–5 m display uncertainty: this is not OSTN15 surveying accuracy. Analysis, clipping, area, chainage, profiles and saved setup remain in EPSG:27700 metres. No vertical datum transformation is made. GeoJSON exports use WGS84 longitude/latitude; projected coordinates and setup are included as properties. Do not set out construction from these coordinates.

Save, compare and export

Drawing and settings save automatically in this browser (localStorage, schema v1). Save setup downloads a portable JSON backup with projected coordinates. Import restores it. Changing geometry or settings clears stale analyses. Candidates and selected footprints are calculated in a worker from static terrain tiles. Cached tiles are kept for the session. CSV contains every candidate × level × sensitivity result, source hash and settings. GeoJSON includes candidate scenario arrays, study geometry and the currently selected footprint. Print report contains a projected terrain map, chosen footprint, comparison table, profiles, sensitivity curves, source and limitations. Numbers in the interface are rounded; exports preserve reproducible calculated values.

Context and constraints

OpenStreetMap raster tiles provide optional buildings, roads, river and bridge context where mapped. They are community-sourced, can be stale or incomplete and may fail to load offline. They are not an asset survey or a comprehensive constraint inventory. The tool does not claim absence of assets or perform proximity clearance. Constraint status remains unknown. Raster provenance and redistribution licence have not been established; the source owner should retain its licence record.

Primary sources and methodological basis

This simplified method is our screening implementation, not an EA or RRC endorsed siting model. Evidence supports examining floodplain connection and site-specific uncertainty; it does not validate a flat-surface map as a hydraulic prediction.

  • Environment Agency: Working with natural processes to reduce flood risk 2024 (published 12 February 2025), and river and floodplain management summaries. Context on leaky barriers, connectivity, varied performance and evidence gaps.
  • River Restoration Centre: Manual of River Restoration Techniques. Practical restoration context and the importance of site-appropriate design.
  • NOAA: Mapping Inundation Uncertainty (2010). Basis for treating elevation and surface uncertainty explicitly in connected terrain inundation screening. Wodah's ±error cases are deterministic sensitivity tests rather than NOAA's probabilistic uncertainty method.
  • USGS: LiDAR projects and topographic versus bathymetric LiDAR. Topographic infrared LiDAR does not establish submerged bed elevations.
  • Ordnance Survey: A guide to coordinate systems in Great Britain and OSTN15/OSGM15 accuracy. Distinguishes approximate Helmert mapping from grid-based transformations.
  • OpenStreetMap contributors — context map attribution and licence.
